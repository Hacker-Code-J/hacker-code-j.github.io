# P-256 implementation audit

This file records the result of using the bignum-arithmetic and elliptic-arithmetic articles as implementation notes for a NIST P-256 arithmetic test library. The folder is internal only and is intentionally not linked from the article index.

## Result

P-256 was implementable as a correctness-first C test artifact with eight little-endian `uint32_t` words per field element and no wider C integer type. The implementation passed deterministic vectors generated by SageMath for field arithmetic, Montgomery encode/decode/multiply, affine validation, Jacobian doubling, mixed addition, inverse-point behavior, and scalar multiplication.

The resulting code is not production-quality cryptographic code. It is a test of implementability and article sufficiency.

## What was sufficient

- The bignum value-map, carry, borrow, comparison, and canonical-residue discussion gave enough structure for fixed-size field elements.
- The schoolbook multiplication article gave the two-word product and scratch-buffer discipline needed for a reviewable product routine.
- The modular arithmetic and Montgomery articles supplied the range-invariant vocabulary for encode/decode/multiply tests.
- The elliptic field-interface article matched the layer boundary used here: field operations below affine/Jacobian point operations.
- The Jacobian article gave enough formulas for P-256 doubling with `a = -3` and mixed affine-Jacobian addition.
- The scalar multiplication article supplied the simple public-scalar double-and-add invariant used in the test harness.

## Gaps found

- The bignum series now uses 32-bit public words as the teaching baseline. The schoolbook multiplication article shows how a 32-by-32 product is decomposed into four 16-by-16 products using only `uint32_t`.
- The elliptic Jacobian formulas need an explicit aliasing contract in C. A note was added to the Jacobian article: clarity-first formulas should be treated as out-of-place unless alias safety has been proved for the assignment schedule.
- The articles were sufficient for correctness-first scalar multiplication, but not enough for a production constant-time scalar routine. Secret-scalar table selection, complete exception handling, and compiler-level leakage checks remain separate work.

## Product decomposition

Each field word product writes

$$
x=(x_0+x_1 2^{16}),\qquad y=(y_0+y_1 2^{16})
$$

and computes four 16-by-16 products. The low output word is assembled from `p00` and the low half of the middle carry. The high output word is assembled from `p11`, the high halves of `p01` and `p10`, and the high half of the middle carry. All intermediate values stay within `uint32_t`.

The test `p256_mul_words(0xffffffff, 0xffffffff)` checks the maximal word product and expects low word `0x00000001` and high word `0xfffffffe`.

## Reduction choice

The normal field multiplication uses a bit-serial modular reduction that maintains a nine-word remainder and subtracts the P-256 prime whenever the remainder crosses the modulus. This is deliberately slow but easy to audit.

A Montgomery REDC path is also implemented for encode/decode/multiply tests. For P-256, the low word of the modulus is `0xffffffff`, so the REDC multiplier is the current low word. The tests encode two field elements, multiply them in Montgomery form, decode the result, and compare it against SageMath multiplication.

## Point-operation assumptions

- Field elements are expected to be canonical at public API validation boundaries.
- The point at infinity is represented in Jacobian coordinates by `Z = 0`.
- Jacobian doubling handles infinity and `Y = 0`.
- Mixed addition handles infinity, equal-point, and inverse-point cases.
- Point-operation outputs should not alias inputs unless the assignment schedule has been separately checked.
- Scalar multiplication is for public deterministic tests, not secret scalars.

## Remaining production gaps

- Replace bit-serial reduction with a reviewed fast P-256 reduction or a fully Montgomery field representation.
- Convert scalar multiplication to a secret-scalar constant-time routine with fixed table access.
- Add systematic randomized differential tests, fuzzing, sanitizers, and cross-compiler checks.
- Review compiler output and memory access patterns for side-channel behavior.
- Add protocol-level ECDH/ECDSA validation around the arithmetic layer instead of treating arithmetic as a protocol.
