## Bridge from closed forms: additive periods become multiplicative monodromy

The exponential sequence is the multiplicative version of the closed-versus-exact story. A positive-degree closed form may have local primitives whose differences are locally constant. A nowhere-zero holomorphic function may have local logarithms whose differences lie in $$2\pi i\mathbb Z$$; after writing the exponential as $$\exp(2\pi i\,\cdot)$$, those differences become integers.

<div class="sc-example" markdown="1">
<span class="sc-env-title">Example: $$d\theta$$ and $$\log z$$</span>

On $$\mathbb C^*$$,

$$
{dz\over z}=d\log z
$$

locally. Around one loop, $$\log z$$ changes by $$2\pi i$$. Applying the exponential removes the local ambiguity but leaves an integral global obstruction.
</div>

<div class="sc-example" markdown="1">
<span class="sc-env-title">Example: from periods to Chern class</span>

For a line bundle with transition functions $$g_{ij}$$, local logarithms $$f_{ij}$$ satisfy integral jumps on triple overlaps. These integers are the Cech representative of $$c_1$$, just as periods of closed forms represent de Rham classes.
</div>
