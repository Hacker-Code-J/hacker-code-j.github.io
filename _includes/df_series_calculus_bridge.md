<span class="df-env-title">Unifying calculus root</span>

The series is organized as a branching of single-variable calculus. The seed is

$$
df=f'(x)\,dx,\qquad \int_a^b df=f(b)-f(a).
$$

From this seed, $$dx$$ becomes a covector, substitution becomes the wedge product and Jacobian determinant, antiderivatives become exact 1-forms, integration by parts becomes Stokes' theorem, and closed-but-not-exact forms become the first visible cohomological obstruction. This last point is the bridge to [Sheaf Cohomology 10](/articles/sc-10-fine-sheaves-and-de-rham/), where de Rham cohomology is presented as closed forms modulo exact forms and then as constant-sheaf cohomology.
